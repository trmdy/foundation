REFERENCE
Message delivery

While a bee is running, never interrupts it. Your message joins the bee's queue and is delivered the moment the current turn ends — the row sits under the composer with a quiet queued tag until then.

is the impatient path: it interrupts the live turn and delivers your message immediately. The queue is untouched — everything already waiting keeps waiting.

Each queued row carries two affordances: send now (interrupt and deliver that row only) and ✕ (cancel before delivery). Rows are editable until they leave the queue.

Bees on an HSR substrate also accept next-tool delivery — the message interjects at the live turn's next tool boundary instead of waiting for the turn to end. Where the substrate can't hold that, Apiary downgrades it to a plain queue append.

OPEN & ENTRY
opens it — matched on the produced character (event.key === '?'), never the physical ⇧/ — so it works on Nordic and every other layout, and never collides with search. Outside editable targets only; a textarea keeps its question marks.
Other doors: a "Keyboard shortcuts ?" row in ⌘K · the palette footer hint · pane ⋯ menu · Help in the app menu. Repeat while open → jumps to the shortcuts entry re-scoped to the pane under the dialog; if already there, closes.
INSIDE THE DIALOG
search is focused on open · type = filter · move rows · run the command (closing the dialog) or open the topic · hop rail ⇄ content · previous · next section · clears the query first, closes second · click keys → record a rebinding.
No j/k inside the dialog — the field owns the keys (same rule as every popover, brief §11.5).
DATA CONTRACT
One source of truth: app commands come from the CommandRegistry (title · category · when · keysFor overrides); pane vocabularies (list spine, diff atlas, composer mnemonics) are contributed by their surface as static atlas blocks. Nothing here is hand-maintained prose — add a command, it appears; remap a key, it updates live.
Context = focusedPaneId → pane kind → section map. Unknown pane kinds simply pin nothing.
